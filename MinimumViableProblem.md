Source https://www.101ways.com/minimum-valuable-problem/

**Key idea**
People appear more willing to clarify and refine a problem than they are to clarify what they mean by "the product".  

**Quote from article**
"When people talk about the product, in my experience, everyone in the room will happily carry the conversation forward, each referring to the product with no one clarifying precisely what they mean.

When people talk about the problem we intend the product to be used to help solve, it is common for the conversation to reiterate, refine, or at least reference which problem we’re speaking about.

I don’t know why these play out in different ways, but they seem to do so.  Perhaps we’ve got a cognitive psychologist in the audience who can shed some light?

Regardless, the minimum valuable problem seems to be something people are comfortable clarifying in conversation."

*Personal note: it is worth me introducing the term within the capability needs / user stories work. 
*

**Related concepts** 

- Minimum Valuable Problem (MVP): strongest if intent is alignment around need, outcomes, and context.
- Thinnest Viable Product: useful when discussing delivery scope and experimentation.
- Thinnest Viable Architecture: useful when discussing technical strategy and avoiding premature design.
- Minimum Valuable Architecture: another possibility if you want to emphasize preserving essential architectural qualities while remaining lean.
